## Two words, two scopes
The difference is the purpose of the work, not the rank of the person.

| | Nuad Thai practitioner | Nuad Thai therapist |
|---|---|---|
| Type of Nuad Thai | Nuad Thai for health promotion | Nuad Thai therapy |
| Purpose | Relaxation, wellbeing, everyday care | Addressing a specific condition |
| Where in Thailand | Licensed health establishments: massage shops and spas | Thai traditional medicine settings: clinics and hospitals |
| Regulated by (Thailand) | Health Establishment Act B.E. 2559 (2016); service providers must register | Thai traditional medicine professional licensing [ต้องตรวจ] |
| What the client should expect | Skilled care for the body, no medical claim | Treatment within a clinical scope |

Why does this matter? In Thailand, a health establishment should not present itself as treating disease. When a shop calls every staff member a "therapist", the word can suggest treatment that the service does not offer. *Practitioner* keeps the promise honest, and it still sounds professional. In English, the same word is used for medical practitioners and nurse practitioners.

## What about Thai professionals working abroad?
This was my first worry too. In many countries, *massage therapist* is a legal title. It comes with a licence, a scope of work and a pay scale. Telling those professionals to drop the word would harm them, and that is not the point.

So the rule is simple. **Abroad, the local legal title comes first.** *Nuad Thai practitioner* is then added as a heritage title, not as a replacement:

> Licensed Massage Therapist · Nuad Thai Practitioner

Read that way, the term adds value. It tells a client in Houston, Berlin or Dubai that this person carries a tradition recognised by UNESCO. A clear heritage title helps clients understand what they are booking.

## How to use the words
Clear words help clients, and they help the people who do the work. Here is how we suggest using them.
- **Spa and massage businesses in Thailand:** call your Nuad Thai team *Nuad Thai practitioners*. Keep *therapist* for services that are truly therapeutic and delivered within that scope.
- **Clinics and hospitals offering Nuad Thai therapy:** *Nuad Thai therapist* fits the work.
- **Spas outside Nuad Thai:** *spa therapist* remains the common international term, and that is fine.
- **Professionals abroad:** keep your local licensed title and add *Nuad Thai practitioner* as your heritage title.
- **Media and writers:** use *Nuad Thai* on first mention, followed by "traditional Thai massage". It is the name the world recognised.

## Quick FAQ
**Is "Nuad Thai" the same as "Thai massage"?** Yes. *Nuad Thai* is the Thai name and the name UNESCO used when it inscribed the practice in 2019. "Traditional Thai massage" is the English description.

**Is a Nuad Thai practitioner less qualified than a therapist?** No. The terms describe the type of work, health promotion or therapy, not the level of skill.

**Can a spa still use the word "therapist"?** Yes. No one is banning a word. We suggest matching the word to the service, so clients know what to expect.

**What should a Thai professional abroad put on a business card?** The local licensed title first, then *Nuad Thai practitioner* as a heritage title.

**Why should Thailand suggest English terms at all?** Nuad Thai is Thai heritage, nominated by Thailand. Clear naming of our own heritage protects its value for everyone who practises it, at home and abroad.

## Closing line (takeaway)
Until we agree, the next time you read a menu or write a job post, ask yourself: what kind of care is really being offered here? Choose the word that tells the truth.

## How I would choose
If I had to propose a Thai pair, I would test each word against four questions:
1. Does it match the scope of the work: health promotion or therapy?
2. Does it sit comfortably with Thai law?
3. Does it honour the person as a bearer of a living heritage?
4. Does it translate cleanly into the English pair Sunai proposed?

| Scope | English | My suggested Thai | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nuad Thai for health promotion | Nuad Thai practitioner | *nak nuad Thai* นักนวดไทย | The prefix *nak* names a person by what they dedicate themselves to, as in *nak dontri* (musician) or *nak kila* (athlete). It signals a profession without a medical claim, and without reducing the person to an employee. |
| Nuad Thai therapy | Nuad Thai therapist | *mor nuad Thai* หมอนวดไทย | It returns the word to its historical meaning: a healer. Because *mor* implies treatment, it should be used only where the person is licensed for therapy under Thai traditional medicine law. |

The other words still have their place. *Phu hai borikan* stays in legal documents. *Phanakngan* stays in employment contracts. *Chang nuad* will live on in everyday speech, and that is fine. My suggestion is only about the word we use when we want one clear reference.

Two cautions. First, many people today use *mor nuad* for everyone, so narrowing it to therapy would take time and goodwill. Second, *nak nuad Thai* is my proposal, not an established term. I have not checked how practitioners themselves feel about it, and their view matters more than mine.

## Why one clear reference matters
For people, a clear word sets the right expectation. A client knows whether they are booking care or treatment. A practitioner can explain who they are without borrowing someone else's title.

For machines, it matters even more than most people realise. AI systems and translation tools learn from what is published. Today, the published record about these words is scattered across ministries, adverts, dictionaries and app translations, so we reflect that mix back. When an industry publishes one clear, well-sourced reference, that reference becomes part of what the next generation of tools learns from.

In other words, the words Thailand agrees on now will shape how the world's machines describe Nuad Thai for years. That is a good reason to choose them carefully, and to choose them together.
